Practical Tips for Delivery
The 10-minute target is the other major issue. With 14 slides, your pacing should be roughly:

Title + motivation: 1 minute
ER theory + 2 simulation slides: 3 minutes
WS theory + simulation slide: 2 minutes
BA theory + simulation slide: 2 minutes
Comparison dashboard + conclusions: 2 minutes

When presenting a simulation figure, always follow this three-beat structure: (1) state what you simulated and the parameters, (2) point to what the plot shows, (3) connect it back to the theorem it confirms or the research question it answers. That keeps you mathematically grounded rather than just describing graphics.
The research questions your dissertation explicitly answers are strong anchor points: RQ1 (ER phase transition threshold), RQ2 (WS small-world window boundaries C(β)/C(0) > 0.5 and L(β)/L(0) < 0.5), and RQ3 (BA power-law exponent τ = 3 independent of m). Stating these explicitly in your conclusion slide and connecting them to your simulation figures will significantly strengthen both the mathematical content and communication marks.
