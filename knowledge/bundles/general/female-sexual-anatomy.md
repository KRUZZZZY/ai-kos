Modern imaging (MRI with fat-saturation sequences) reveals the clitoris as a 9-11cm three-dimensional erectile tissue complex — only the glans (~10% of mass) is externally visible. Internal structures include the boomerang-shaped body (paired corpora cavernosa), bilateral crura following the ischiopubic rami, and vestibular bulbs flanking the vaginal wall. The suspensory ligament has superficial and deep components anchoring to the pubic symphysis. The dorsal nerve of the clitoris (DNC) carries ~5,140 myelinated axons per side (~10,280 bilateral), with additional unmyelinated and autonomic fibers — a receptor density comparable to the median nerve of the hand concentrated in a much smaller area, explaining susceptibility to overstimulation. The urethrovaginal locus (5-8cm depth) is a convergence zone of internal clitoral bulbs, urethral sponge (PDE5-containing erectile tissue), and Skene's glands (female prostate, producing PSA/PAP). Female ejaculation (small volume, milky, from Skene's glands) is distinct from squirting (large volume, clear/diluted, from bladder). The autonomic nervous system requires parasympathetic dominance for arousal and sympathetic activation for orgasm — stress inhibits the response via sympathetic overdrive.
